# 03 — Scientific Evidence Framework

**Purpose:** Keep Brain Clean honest. Prevent Lumosity-style overclaim and invented clinical metrics.

---

## Evidence hierarchy (strongest → weakest)

1. Meta-analysis of RCTs  
2. Systematic review  
3. Individual RCTs (preregistered preferred)  
4. Quasi-experimental / strong observational with controls  
5. Observational / correlational  
6. Mechanistic neuroscience without behavioral outcome  
7. Expert consensus / clinical guidelines (context-dependent)  
8. Uncontrolled pre-post / product anecdotes  
9. Marketing claims / influencer content  

**Label every claim with best available tier.** If unknown: **Evidence verification required.**

---

## Confidence score (0–100)

| Score | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 90–100 | Multiple high-quality RCTs/meta-analyses; clear effect; relevant population |
| 70–89 | Good RCT/systematic review support; some limits (population/size) |
| 50–69 | Mixed evidence or plausible mechanism + limited trials |
| 30–49 | Weak/indirect evidence; hypothesis-grade for product |
| 10–29 | Anecdote / speculative neuroscience popularization |
| 0–9 | Contradicted or marketing-only |

Adjust down for: tiny samples, no replication, wrong population, surrogate endpoints, conflicts of interest, overgeneralized “brain” language.

---

## Rules by study type

| Type | Product use |
|------|-------------|
| Meta-analysis / systematic review | Preferred basis for strong copy |
| RCT | Strong for specific intervention claims |
| Observational | Can motivate features; cannot prove causation in marketing |
| Expert opinion | Useful for safety/tone; weak for efficacy claims |
| Anecdotes | UX inspiration only |
| Marketing claims | Never import as science |

---

## Replication & sample size

- Prefer replicated findings.  
- Small n + large effect → skepticism.  
- Lab-only tasks ≠ real-life phone use outcomes unless linked.

---

## Correlation vs causation

Allowed: “Associated with…”, “People who… often…”  
Forbidden without causal evidence: “Improves IQ”, “Cures brain fog”, “Repairs dopamine receptors”, “Scientifically measures mental clarity” as clinical fact.

---

## Domain-specific guardrails

### Focus / attention
Prefer: brief rests, mindfulness, exercise, sleep, reducing interruptions.  
Avoid: claiming game score = life focus.

### Memory
Prefer: sleep, spaced practice, encoding strategies.  
Avoid: “brain training games transfer to memory” without strong evidence.

### Sleep
Strong practical domain; still no medical diagnosis of sleep disorders.

### “Dopamine detox”
**PRODUCT DECISION:** Do not market as scientific detox of dopamine. Prefer “reduce compulsive use / reclaim attention.”

### Stress / anxiety
Tools may support calm; **no diagnosis or treatment claims.** Escalate crisis language carefully (see Safa framework).

### Brain fog
Treat as user-language symptom cluster, not a clinical diagnosis Brain Clean treats.

### Screen use
Reducing compulsive use can be beneficial; avoid moral panic claims without evidence.

### Cognitive training / games
**FACT (industry):** Brain-training transfer claims have faced regulatory action historically (e.g., Lumosity FTC case — verify citation before public legal claims).  
**PRODUCT DECISION:** Do not sell mini-games as intelligence enhancement or brain-fog cure. Optional: impulse-pause / playful break framing only.

### BCI / invented indices
**FACT (master doc intent):** BCI described as internally invented motivational index, not medical measure.  
**PRODUCT DECISION:** Never present as clinical brain measurement. Prefer behavior-based labels (consistency, completion).

---

## Medical-claim guardrails

Forbidden phrases (examples): diagnose, treat, cure, therapy, clinical proven brain repair, ADHD treatment, antidepressant alternative.

Allowed: digital wellbeing, habits, attention routines, calm tools, self-reflection — with disclaimers.

---

## Scientific copywriting rules

1. Match claim strength to evidence tier.  
2. Prefer concrete behaviors (“I drank water”) over abstract %.  
3. Explain metrics: why + what to do next.  
4. Separate **app score** from **health**.  
5. When unsure: soften or omit.

### Disclaimer patterns (templates)

**EN:** “Brain Clean supports healthy digital habits. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat any condition.”  

**AR:** «Brain Clean يدعم عادات رقمية صحية. ليس جهازًا طبيًا ولا يشخص أو يعالج أي حالة.»

## Claim examples

| Claim | Status |
|-------|--------|
| “Complete a short daily routine to build intentional habits.” | **Accepted** (habit framing) |
| “Wakeful rest / calm breaks may support attention for some people.” | **Softened** — Evidence verification required for specific effect sizes |
| “BCI scientifically measures your mental clarity.” | **Rejected** |
| “Our games cure brain fog / raise IQ.” | **Rejected** |
| “Writing worries can help some people feel clearer.” | **Softened** — CBT-adjacent; no treatment claim |
| “Safa diagnoses your anxiety type.” | **Rejected** |

---

## Process

Before shipping copy or AI prompts that assert science:

1. Identify claim  
2. Assign evidence tier + confidence  
3. Choose Accepted / Softened / Rejected  
4. Record in knowledge graph (`13`)
